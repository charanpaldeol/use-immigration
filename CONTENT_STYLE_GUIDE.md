# Content Style Guide
## Immigration Services Business - Niagara

## Brand Voice
- Professional, precise, and approachable
- Action-oriented: always include practical next steps
- Calm and trustworthy; avoid fear-based persuasion
- Never promise guaranteed immigration outcomes

## Tone and Language Rules
- Use plain language for legal/process topics
- Prefer short paragraphs (2-4 sentences)
- Define acronyms on first use (e.g., Ontario Immigrant Nominee Program)
- Include dates and context when discussing policy updates

## Formatting Standards
- Use clear H1 > H2 > H3 hierarchy
- Use bullets for 3+ related points
- Use simple comparison tables for pathway choices
- Keep section intros concise and scannable
- Include at least one checklist in each long guide

## CTA Standards
- Minimum 2 CTA moments per long-form guide
- Preferred CTA verbs:
  - Book Consultation
  - Check Eligibility
  - Request Assessment
- Keep CTA labels short and specific

## Data and Source Standards
- Validate program information before publishing
- Link to official government resources when possible
- Mark assumptions as estimates, not facts
- Refresh policy-sensitive pages on a scheduled cadence

## Niagara Integration Standard
- Mention Niagara in strategic context, not keyword stuffing
- Include regional labor-market or settlement relevance
- Compare regional practical factors where useful (cost, opportunity, community)
- Keep references specific to user decisions and pathway fit

## SEO Standards
- Target keyword in title and intro
- Include one secondary keyword family in headings
- Add internal links to related guides and core service pages
- Keep metadata concise, accurate, and non-clickbait

## Compliance Standard
- Include disclaimers where legal interpretation may be inferred
- Avoid claims without supporting evidence
- Protect client privacy in all examples and case references
